Every "best time to post" chart on the internet is a generic average pulled from someone else's
audience. It's a reasonable starting point, not a rule — a B2B LinkedIn audience and a gaming
Discord-adjacent TikTok audience are not awake at the same hours. Treat everything below as a
starting window to test, not a schedule to follow blindly.

## General windows worth testing

- **Instagram & Facebook** — late morning (10am–12pm) and early evening (6pm–8pm) on weekdays
  tend to outperform late night, since most usage clusters around commute and after-work hours.
- **LinkedIn** — weekday mornings (8am–10am), Tuesday through Thursday. Weekend LinkedIn traffic
  is real but noticeably thinner.
- **TikTok** — evenings, especially 7pm–10pm, when people are scrolling to unwind rather than
  during a work-day break.
- **YouTube** — a few hours before peak evening viewing (early-to-mid afternoon) so the algorithm
  has time to start surfacing a new upload before the evening rush.
- **Mastodon & Bluesky** — both skew toward tech-adjacent, terminally-online audiences with less
  pronounced peaks than the algorithmic platforms above; early morning and evening both perform
  reasonably, and there's more tolerance for odd-hour posts than on Instagram or TikTok.
- **Pinterest** — evenings and weekends, since it's a planning/browsing platform more than a
  real-time one — pins also have a much longer shelf life than a tweet or a Story.

## Why your own data beats a generic chart

None of the above accounts for your specific audience's timezone distribution, industry, or
habits. The fastest way to find your actual best time is to post at a few different windows for
a couple of weeks and compare what happened — not what you assume happened.
