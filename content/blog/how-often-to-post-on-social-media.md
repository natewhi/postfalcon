There's no single right answer here, and anyone who gives you an exact number without asking
about your audience first is guessing. What does matter, consistently, across every platform: a
predictable cadence you can actually sustain beats a burst of ten posts followed by three weeks
of silence.

## Rough starting ranges

- **Instagram & Facebook** — 3–5 posts a week is a reasonable baseline for most accounts; daily
  is fine if you have the content, but quality drops fast when frequency is the only goal.
- **LinkedIn** — 2–4 posts a week tends to outperform daily posting for most company and personal
  brand accounts — the algorithm and the audience both reward less, more considered content here.
- **TikTok** — the platform explicitly rewards frequency; 1 post a day is a common recommendation
  among creators who treat it seriously, since each video is judged mostly on its own merits.
- **YouTube** — driven far more by watch time and production quality than raw frequency; a
  consistent weekly upload beats a rushed daily one.
- **Mastodon & Bluesky** — closer to a real-time feed than a curated one; multiple posts a day is
  completely normal and rarely penalized the way it might be elsewhere.
- **Pinterest** — pins have a long shelf life, so a steady handful per week compounds over time
  more than a single burst.

## Consistency is the actual lever

A schedule you can keep is worth more than an ambitious one you abandon after two weeks. Pick a
frequency you're confident you can sustain for a quarter, not the maximum you could theoretically
manage this week.

## Making "consistent" not require willpower

The easiest way to keep a cadence going without relying on remembering to do it is to schedule
ahead of time. [Recurring posts](/blog/recurring-social-media-posts) cover anything on a fixed
loop — daily, weekly, or monthly — and batching a week or two of one-off posts into the
[composer](/blog/how-to-schedule-a-post) in one sitting means the schedule keeps running even on
the days you don't open the app at all.
